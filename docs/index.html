What the number is made of ↓

Ten domains

Fifty measured indicators, grouped. Each is scored against a stated healthy baseline and a stated collapse threshold. The second column is how much of the total weight that domain carries.

How the weighting works

A hazard that can cross its entire range before anyone reacts matters more than one of equal size that takes forty years to arrive. So weights are derived from four scored axes rather than assigned by hand.

S · Severity ceiling

How much of civilization this could end at its worst. Nuclear exchange scores 5. Orbital debris scores 1.

V · Velocity

How fast it can travel from here to catastrophic. 5 is minutes. 1 is decades. Launch orders and carbon curves sit at opposite ends.

I · Irreversibility

Whether the catastrophic state can be undone. Extinction is permanent. Inflation is not.

C · Coupling

How strongly failure here drags other domains down with it.

weight ∝ S1.0 × V0.8 × I0.4 × C0.6

Velocity carries the second-largest exponent deliberately. The result puts 26.6% of all weight on the nuclear domain and 0.28% on inflation. Switching to equal weight moves the headline from 53% to 45% — the clearest evidence that weighting is a real decision and not a formality.

How this is built, and what it isn't

The discourse chose the topics. The data set the scores. Fifty subjects were drawn from what is actually being argued about globally. Scoring then came from measured indicators with named sources, because how alarmed an article sounds is not a measurement.

Every anchor is a judgment. Each indicator needs a value for healthy and a value for finished. Some rest on physics — coral stops accreting below about 5% cover, the Amazon's hydrological cycle fails near 25% clearance. Others, like biodiversity or novel chemical entities, have no threshold anyone can point to. Those are marked.

The weighting is derived but the axis scores are not. The formula is mechanical; the S, V, I and C values feeding it are considered judgments. Both views are on this page so the difference is visible.

A single number compresses away everything that matters. 53% is not a forecast. It says that across fifty measured things, weighted by how fast and how permanently each could go wrong, the world sits just past halfway between a healthy state and a collapsed one. The breakdown is the content; the headline is a handle.

Known weak points. The Living Planet Index is contested and may overstate decline, and it is one of the highest scores here. Several figures in fast-moving areas come from headline-level reporting rather than primary data. The nuclear domain partly double-counts, since the Doomsday Clock already folds climate and biological risk into its own setting.
